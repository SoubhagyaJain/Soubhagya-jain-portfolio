Cover, research notebook: Why does multilingual RAG miss answers in another language? A German query, "Wie funktioniert ein Transformer?", retrieves a same-language German passage while the best English answer is not in the top-k. Soubhagya Jain, open the notes.
1, What I assumed: in a multilingual model, language should drop out — "Reset my password" in English, Hindi and German land in one cluster. Meaning decides position; language shouldn't.
2, What can happen: vector ≈ meaning + language + model geometry. Recall@10 is 0.61 when the relevant answer is in the query's language and 0.08 when an equally relevant answer is in another language (multilingual-e5-small, XRAG, 15k news articles in 5 languages). Same relevance, very different recall.
3, Colour vs shape: an ideal plot clusters by topic; the warning sign is clusters by language. An illustration, not proof — the retrieval metrics are the evidence.
4, Why RAG breaks: query, embedding, ANN top-k, then reranker, LLM, answer. Language shapes the vector and is locked in at top-k; the reranker only sees top-k. The LLM can only ground on what top-k lets through.
5, Rerankers can't rescue: one Spanish query with its English answer at rank #438, while the reranker scores the top 50. Recall@20 low means a candidate problem — fix retrieval first; Recall@20 fine with MRR low means a ranking problem, where a reranker helps. Candidate recall is not ranking.
6, The SHIFT idea: training-free, applied at index time. Estimate a language direction from a few thousand translation pairs, subtract a scaled version from each embedding. An intuition, not a clean linear decomposition; the effect varies by model.
7, My experiment: a Hindi query about how transformers work, with a relevant English passage and a weaker Hindi distractor. Which one ranks first? Repeated across 200 queries, reporting Recall@5/10/20, MRR and nDCG@10, always split into same-language and cross-language.
8, The trade-off: after SHIFT, cross-language Recall@10 goes from 0.08 to 0.24 and same-language from 0.61 to 0.49 (Qdrant, multilingual-e5-small, XRAG). When query and answer share a language, bias may be harmless; with mostly-English corpora, invariance is critical. Where does the answer live?
9, The principle: before choosing an embedding model, measure where your queries live, where your evidence lives, and whether language became a ranking feature nobody chose. "A multilingual model is not a language-neutral retriever by default." Sources: Jang, Hong, Moon and Lim, SHIFT, EMNLP 2026 Findings; Qdrant, SHIFTing languages in multilingual RAG.
